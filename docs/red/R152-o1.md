## 2. The exact reason (PROVED, lane; L-RED successor 36)
Fix a W-run F with τ and λ_W fixed; c := λ_W(X), X ∉ C. Premises: ARENA-MAX and W-MON (P-N5-SETTLE §4, signed), Claim RG (T54 §4, a READING
there; P-N5-SETTLE §3 Statement proves its form for any transition), IDX-MONO and LEX-BFS (T54 §4.1-§4.2, used in P-N5-SETTLE Case 2a).
- Lemma TIE-W. If W_(k+1)(v) = W_k(v) for an Even v ∉ C, then every vertex on v's (σ_k, τ)-path keeps its W at k → k + 1, and every
  σ_k-edge of that path is W_(k+1)-tight.
  - Proof. Put w := σ_k(v). If v does not switch, then W_(k+1)(v) = v ⊕ W_(k+1)(w) and W_k(v) = v ⊕ W_k(w); v ⊕ · reflects ⊴, so
    W_(k+1)(w) = W_k(w). If v switches, the hypotheses are those of RUN-SETTLE Case 2 with y := v, a := w («W_(k+1)(y) = W_k(y)»):
    if w's (σ_(k+1), τ)-path avoids v, the chain of Case 2b gives W_(k+1)(w) = W_k(w) (tight); if it contains v, Case 2c's argument
    gives a contradiction. Neither case uses the event-free hypothesis or S_(j−1); they use only ARENA-MAX at k → k + 1 and W-MON.
    Induct along the path (each vertex keeps W, so the next one does). □
- Corollary TIE. A switch a → b of v with W(v) unchanged has a and b both W_(k+1)-tight at v (a by TIE-W; b as σ_(k+1)(v)).
- Lemma O1-ODD. If Ω(c) is odd, O1 holds for X.
  - Proof. Suppose W(X) is constant on [k, j + 1]. Every switch of X in this interval is a tie (Corollary TIE), and σ_k(X) is in the arena
    and survives (Claim RG). By IDX-MONO the new successor has a strictly smaller index than the old one. So index(σ(X)) strictly
    decreases along the switches in [k, j + 1]; X cannot re-enter g. □
- Lemma KEY-MON (extends T54's LEX-MONO from flat intervals to one vertex with W fixed in any W-run). If Ω(c) is even and
  W_(k+1)(v) = W_k(v), then key_(σ_(k+1))(v) ≤ key_(σ_k)(v) lexicographically, strictly if v switches. (key as T54 §4.2: the vertices of
  v's (σ, τ)-path read from c.)
  - Proof. Let v = p_0, …, p_d = c be the (σ_k, τ)-path. By TIE-W each p_i keeps W and p_(i+1) is W_(k+1)-tight at p_i. Induct from
    i = d. Odd p_i: τ fixed, key_(k+1)(p_i) = (key_(k+1)(p_(i+1)), p_i). Even p_i: by LEX-BFS σ_(k+1)(p_i) is the lex-least key among
    the tight arena successors, and p_(i+1) = σ_k(p_i) is one of them. So key_(k+1)(p_i) ≤ (key_(k+1)(p_(i+1)), p_i) ≤ (key_k(p_(i+1)),
    p_i) = key_k(p_i). Strict at a switch, because distinct successors have distinct keys. □
- Lemma XI-PAY (elementary; no W hypothesis). In one τ-stretch, if Even X leaves g at k → k + 1 and next enters g at j → j + 1, then
  Ξ_j(g) ▷ Ξ_k(g).
  - Proof. Every non-fallback switch goes to a successor in Iσ (the step-2 arena is {σ(v)} ∪ Iσ(v)); fallback switches are improving
    too. Let g = x_0, x_1, …, x_r = g be X's successors at its switches k = k_1 < … < k_r = j. Then
    Ξ_(k_i)(x_i) ▷ Ξ_(k_i)(x_(i−1)), and Ξ is monotone in a τ-stretch ([R15], as used in RUN-SETTLE Case 1). Chaining:
    Ξ_j(g) = Ξ_(k_r)(x_r) ▷ Ξ_(k_r)(x_(r−1)) ⊵ Ξ_(k_(r−1))(x_(r−1)) ▷ … ▷ Ξ_(k_1)(x_0) = Ξ_k(g). □
- Theorem O1-SPLIT (the exact reason). Let X ∉ C re-enter g in F with W(X) unchanged (an O1 violation). Then Ω(c) is even, and between
  the two entries:
  (i) key(g) strictly decreases (key_(j+1)(g) < key_k(g)); X's own key strictly decreases at each of its switches;
  (ii) Ξ(g) strictly rises (XI-PAY);
  (iii) W is constant on X's whole path at every transition of the interval (TIE-W), so in particular W(g) is constant.
  - Proof. O1-ODD excludes Ω(c) odd. (iii) is TIE-W. By KEY-MON, key(X) is non-increasing on [k, j + 1] and strictly decreases at k.
    key_k(X) = (key_k(g), X) and key_(j+1)(X) = (key_(j+1)(g), X), so key_(j+1)(g) < key_k(g). (ii) is XI-PAY. □
- So the tie route does NOT need a W-change. It needs a Ξ-rise at the re-entered target (this is generic SI and holds for every
  re-entry) plus a lex-key decrease along W-tight paths. The key is a word of length ℓ ≤ n − 1 over the vertex indices, so KEY-MON alone
  allows exponentially many decreases at one vertex. This is T54 §4.4: in the abstract model LEX-ARENA (adversarial arenas ⊇ {σ(v)}), the
  longest run is 2^(L+1) − 2L − 2 at depth L.

## 4. Consequences for the R* question (READING)
- The R* target («rounds use FRESH W-levels at X», brief (b)) is not the only route to a long W-run. On the EVEN branch, rounds can run on
  fresh lex keys with W frozen along X's path. Each round then costs a Ξ-rise at the re-entered target (XI-PAY), which is the
  XI-CH-POLY residual of R95 again, and not the R* count.
- Concrete next attack (T54 §4.4's template, «Not attempted» there): realise the width-2 LEX-ARENA counter (2^(L+1) − 2L − 2 tie switches
  at depth L) inside one W-run of N5. The arena schedule must come from Ξ-improvement (S2). Each key decrease at depth L needs a tie switch
  at some vertex nearer c whose new successor is Ξ-improving at that moment. The width-2 tight DAG is a W-flat layered gadget (equal
  priorities, irrelevant to c). The Ξ-schedule needs Odd deviators off the DAG whose best replies rise in a counter order.
- On the proof side, O1 should be replaced by O1-SPLIT. A proof of WRUN-POLY through ties needs a bound on lex-key decreases charged to
  Ξ-rises: «a W-flat re-entry of g is paid by a Ξ-rise at g», with Ξ-rises at Even targets counted polynomially. That is XI-CH-POLY.
