## 0. The candidate, as attacked
- Rule (the Lead's sentence, 14:06Z; L-SI's pseudocode had not arrived when this was written): «one-sided, memoryless, inside the classical VJ scheme: in the play graph of (σ, σ̄), an improvable Even vertex v DEPENDS on an improvable u ≠ v iff u lies on the play from σ(v) or from one of v's improving targets; switch-best exactly at the improvable vertices in the SINK strongly connected components of the dependency digraph.» Starts (Lead 14:24Z): ι on Friedmann's G_n, S3's Even part elsewhere; Odd's side is never iterated (σ̄ is the optimal counterstrategy at each iteration).
- My readings (experiments/red/r247/rf247.py header): R1 the play from a target w is the whole play w, nxt(w), … (path and cycle, w included); R2 every improving target counts; R3 a sink component has no edge into another component (a single vertex without dependency is one); R4 switch-best = Ξ-best improving successor, ties to the larger index.
- Unit everywhere: ITERATIONS = applied switch sets until no improving edge is left (the last evaluation is not counted).
- Correctness is not in question: every applied switch is an improving switch of the classical one-sided scheme, so the run terminates in an optimal σ (VJ00); no wrong winner and no non-terminating run was seen (the exhaustive runs of §5 assert termination below 10,000 iterations).

## 2. Where RF leaves Howard's exponential run (DATA + READING)
- viol247.py runs Howard on G_n and asks at every step which improvable vertices RF would not switch. On G_3: 38 of the 62 steps have no blocked vertex (there RF's step is Howard's step); the 24 blocked steps are the RESET steps (Table 2; G_2: 10 of 26; G_4: 52 of 134).
- The cancelled switch (trace247.py on G_2, out/viol.out): when bit j closes, Howard switches d_i → s for the set bits i < j (the reset) and k_i → g_j (the exit's update) in the SAME step. Under RF, d_i depends on k_i, because k_i lies on d_i's CURRENT play d_i → e_i → h_i → k_i; k_i is a dependency sink and moves alone; after it the exit of bit i already sees bit j, and d_i → s is no longer improving. The lane's reset (t_m → s) is cancelled in the same way by c and r moving first. So RF reaches the optimum of G_n without one reset: bit 1 closes, bit 2 closes, …
- READING: every Howard counter of Friedmann's type lives on switches that are improving only with respect to valuations that a pending switch further down the same play is about to change. RF waits for exactly those.

## 3. Fact DECOY (PROVED lane, three lines) and its DATA
The Lead's idea (14:24Z): decorate a Howard-exponential family with decoy successors so that RF's dependency digraph is strongly connected at every step, each decoy «IMPROVING but never the BEST target».
- **Fact DECOY.** Let v be a dependency sink and u a vertex blocked by v because v lies on u's CURRENT play (σ, σ̄). If a target w of v has u on its play, then play(w) = π · v · play(σ(v)) for a path π from w through u to v, so Ξ(w) = π ⊕ v ⊕ Ξ(σ(v)). Hence w is improving iff the most relevant vertex of π ∪ {v} above the cycle node is even (or, if none is above it, by the length rule), i.e. iff switching v to w closes a cycle through v and u whose most relevant vertex is good for Even. Proof: the play from u is u's current play, which reaches v; from v it follows σ(v); the value of a vertex not on the cycle of its own play is the ⊕ of the path to the next vertex (SPEC S1). ∎
- Consequence: such a decoy is never «insignificant». Either Odd cannot leave the closed cycle, and then the decoy is a winning move of Even and the decorated game is no longer the counter; or Odd can leave it, and then the decoy is a real cycle gate: improving only until it is closed, and closed as soon as it is v's only improving target. Decoys can exist only toward a u that is blocked through an improving, NON-current target; none of the pairs that carry Friedmann's counter (d_i / k_i, r / k_i, t / c, t / r: Table 2) is of that kind.
- DATA (Table 3; fvar247.py decoy=all / decoy=1: k_i → z_i → d_i with z_i Even, priority 0, one successor): Even wins 25 / 36 / 47 vertices of G_2 / G_3 / G_4 (0 before); Howard needs 8 / 10 / 12 iterations (26 / 62 / 134 before); RF 11 / 15 / 19.
- Delay patches (Table 4): buffers between g_j and f_j and a lane base without its edge to s leave RF at 5, 9, 12, 15, 18 (n = 1..5). A buffer delays once: after its first switch the chain is transparent and nothing re-arms it.

## 4. What is left open by RF's definition (READING)
- LOOPHOLE: RF waits only for improvable vertices on the plays of the CURRENT and the IMPROVING targets of v. Pending improvements below an option that is not improving now are not waited for; v can switch, and switch again when that option overtakes. A counter for RF would have to live on that staleness.
- BLOCKS: inside one sink component RF is Howard. Mutual dependency through improving targets (v's improving target leads through u and u's through v, neither current play passing the other) needs no winning cycle; climb_r8 (Table 7; out/climbs/best_r8.pg, 8 vertices, 2 Odd) has a vertex that returns 6 times to an edge it used before in a 13-iteration RF run, by leapfrogging inside such a block.
- GENUINE RESETS: a set bit that really becomes worse than leaving once a higher bit is set, and better again later. With exits that see the higher bits, the set state follows them without a reset; with static exits it never becomes attractive again. I found no gadget that does both.
