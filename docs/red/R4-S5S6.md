## Realisability of the published orders by an index order (L-RED reading, agreeing with L-SI's 13:10Z notes)
- An index order is lexicographic in (v, w): the edges of one Even vertex are contiguous, in successor-index order, and the
  within-block orders of all vertices come from one global numbering. Avis-Friedmann's ordering (1) (arXiv 1305.3944 p.13-14,
  read): phase classes {b, d^0, e^1} < {c} < {e^0} < {d^1} < {a}, with e_1^1 < d_2^0 < e_2^1 < b_2^1 < b_2^0 < ... inside
  phase 1; the two edges of every e_i (e_i^1 in phase 1, e_i^0 in phase 3) and of every d_i (d_i^0 in phase 1, d_i^1 in
  phase 4) are in different classes, so (1) is not a block order and no renumbering of G_n realises it. (PGSolver's
  implementation order has the same property, L-SI's observation on e_i.) Theorem 4 (p.14: "using the ordering (1)") does
  not transfer by renumbering.
- Splitting a vertex's choice into separately numbered Even vertices (a chain of binary choosers x_1 -> {w_1, x_2},
  x_2 -> {w_2, x_3}, ...) changes the improvement structure: a chooser behind the current taker compares its edge with its
  own continuation, not with the current choice, so the chain game has improving switches the original lacks and Cunningham's
  run on it is a different run; the gate gadget of R1 keeps the choice at one hub and cannot split it either. A faithful
  splitter would have to make exactly the original edges improving; I see no such gadget.
- DFH's Zadeh tie-break (arXiv 1911.01074 v2 p.3, read: "it cannot be avoided to fix a tie-breaking rule", "conceptually,
  our construction is not based on exploiting the tie-breaking rule") is, per L-SI's reading of section 6.2 (not re-derived
  here), a strategy-dependent pre-order with one explicit exception: not a fixed order, so again not an index order.

## What a kill would need
Either a construction made for block orders (every bit's switches at vertices whose edges may be ordered contiguously),
or a growth proof for a scheme found by search. The permutation searches reach 2/3 of the designer order's count at N = 3 and
less at N = 4, 5 (24/38, 51/121, 74/324), suggesting the block constraint costs a constant factor per level, i.e. the
exponential base, not just a prefactor; a Cunningham counter that is robust to block orders is the open design problem.
