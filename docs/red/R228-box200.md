## 1. Reading
- SELF (the Lead's question: «does a y-top of v really become a top of u at u's own switching step?»): YES.
  - SPLIT (i) gives nx_(t−1)(u) ≠ nx_t(u) = b.
  - TRANSFER (iv) gives Λ_(t−1)(u) = Λ_t(u) = w and the same symmetric difference, hence the same top y.
  - The Λ condition of TRANSFER holds because u lies on v's play at both t − 1 and t (the common prefix), so (PL) gives Λ(u) = Λ(v) = w
    at both. Nothing else is needed.
- TIME-CHARGE: distinct y-tops are distinct steps, each in S_y of its own pair, so the map is an injection. There are ≤ 3M pairs (standard
  states, as in SPLIT-COUNT). For fixed (u, b, w) the sets S_y are disjoint, since one change has one top. ✓
- LEAP:
  - Mover: REWIN (a) with the letter removed, then F5 at the switch t′.
  - Gate: at t Odd plays b, so Ω_t(b) ◁ Ω_t(a) ⊴ Ω_(t′−1)(a) (F1). At t′ − 1 Odd plays a, so Ω_(t′−1)(a) ◁ Ω_(t′−1)(b) (LOC-GATE3, NO-TIE3).
    Both inequalities hold. ✓
- R-EQUIV constants:
  - Every step before the first fallback switches a mover.
  - A mover's Λ changes at most 2M + 2 times (CYC: ≤ 2M + 3 values, increasing reward).
  - Every other switch of x lies in some R(x, b, w). There are M pairs (x, b) (one pad per option) and ≤ 2M + 3 values of w.
  - So T3 ≤ N(2M + 2) + M(2M + 3)·R; R ≤ T3 is trivial. ✓
  - My check reqfail tests this bound with my Γ's M mover edges and R over movers only (Rmov): 0 everywhere, as expected.
