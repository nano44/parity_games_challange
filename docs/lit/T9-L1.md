## (a) Primary source for the µ-calculus characterisation of W_E
ANP (LIPIcs.CSL.2021.9, p.2) cite "Arnold and Niwiński [1, Section 4]": A. Arnold, D. Niwiński, *Rudiments of µ-Calculus*,
Studies in Logic and the Foundations of Mathematics, Elsevier, 2001. **NOT opened.** It is an Elsevier book, no open
copy was located, and L-LIT did not try a library route. Opened substitutes, in order of usefulness:

1. **Bradfield, Walukiewicz, "The mu-calculus and model-checking"** (chapter of the Handbook of Model Checking; author copy
   www.labri.fr/perso/igw/Papers/igw-mu.pdf). Formula (1), p.19 (PDF p.18), in the discussion after Theorem 7. Verbatim except
   for symbol rendering:
   - "let us suppose that the ranks range from 0 to 2d + 1. A game G = ⟨V, V_E, V_A, T ⊆ V × V, Ω : V → {0, . . . , 2d + 1}⟩ can
     be represented as a transition system M_G where V is the set of states, and T is a transition relation on some letter,
     say b. The additional information as to whether a position belongs to V_E, and what is its rank is coded with auxiliary
     propositions: p_Eve, p_Adam, p_0, . . . , p_{2d+1}."
   - "To write a formula defining the winning position we take variables Z_0, . . . , Z_{2d+1}, one for every possible value
     of the rank. The formula is:
     µZ_{2d+1}.νZ_{2d} . . . µZ_1.νZ_0. γ(Z_0, . . . , Z_d) where γ(Z_0, . . . , Z_d) is ⋀_i ( p_i ⇒ (p_Eve ∧ ⟨b⟩Z_i) ∨ (p_Adam ∧ [b]Z_i) )   (1)"
   - Convention (p.13): "Acc = {v0 v1 . . . ∈ V^ω : lim sup Ω(v_i) is even}", i.e. max-parity, as in TARGET.md §1.
   - Status: the formula is UNNUMBERED, stated without proof in the chapter. Theorem 7 ("Equivalence of games and
     model-checking [46, 43]") is the numbered statement it illustrates. Its sources are [46] Emerson, Jutla, FOCS'91
     (NOT opened, IEEE) and [43] Emerson, Jutla, Sistla, TCS 258 (2001).
   - Text typo: the argument list reads "γ(Z_0, . . . , Z_d)" but the variables are Z_0 ... Z_{2d+1}.
2. **Emerson, Jutla, Sistla, "On model checking for the µ-calculus and its fragments"**, TCS 258 (2001) 491-522
   (opened copy: people.irisa.fr/Nicolas.Markey/PDF/Papers/tcs258(1-2)-EJS.pdf).
   - Theorem 3.2 is the closest NUMBERED primary statement: "Given a parity tree automaton A ... we can obtain a Kripke
     structure K whose size is linear in the size of A and a µ-calculus formula f which is linear in the length of the
     acceptance condition; and a state s0 in K; such that • alt depth(f) = 1+ the number of sets in F; and • A accepts at
     least one input iff K, s0 |= f."
   - The formula, p.505: "σ_{k−1}x_{k−1}.σ_{k−2}x_{k−2} · · · νx_0. ⋁_{0≤i<k} (P_i ∧ ⟨R⟩[R]x_i), where σ_{k−1} · · · σ_0 is an alternating
     sequence of µs and νs ending with ν". Lemma 3.5 is the inductive invariant over the prefix g_l(y).
   - It is stated for emptiness of parity tree automata, i.e. for the special one-letter games whose Eve moves (⟨R⟩) choose
     a transition and whose Adam moves ([R]) choose a direction. The parity convention is "maxindex ... is an odd number"
     with G_0, G_1, ... indexing, so it is shifted against TARGET's.

Notes:
- Solving ANP's asymmetric system of |V| least-fixpoint equations obliviously (round-robin Kleene iteration until the
  lattice height n·|V| is exhausted) costs up to |V|^2·n queries. That again gives exponent 2·lg(d/lg n)+O(1).
- So the oblivious upper bound L-LOWER can cite is n^{2·lg(d/lg n)+O(1)} (ANP Lemma 3.2 with p.9:9), and the gap to
  n^{lg(d/lg n)+O(1)} is open as far as L-LIT can see. Whether adaptivity buys the square-root saving in the SLP model is
  itself a clean question.
