## 1. What the algorithm is (as written, no judgement on correctness)
- Convention: **min-parity** ("lowest priority encountered infinitely often", §1, §2). TARGET.md is max-parity.
  Translation for implementers: p' = M - p with M even and M >= max p (keeps parity, reverses order).
- Self-loops: assumed absent; a self-loop-removal pre-routine is cited to Friedmann-Lange ATVA 2009 [FL09]
  (DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-04761-9_15; L-LIT has NOT opened FL09). Most Oink/PGSolver families contain self-loops,
  so the implementation's pre-routine is part of the algorithm and must be in the pseudocode.
- Core operator A(G,d) (§3, p.3-4): for player P = par(d),
  U_d = vertices of P's parity with priority <= d; A*_d = vertices of priority k (opposite parity, k <= d) in
  P-Attr(G, U_{k-1}); A'_d(G,A) = (opponent)-Attr of {v in A \ (A*_d ∪ U_d) : pr(v) < d};
  U^0 = U_d, A^0 = P-Attr(U^0); U^{k+1} = U^k \ (opponent)-Attr((V \ A^k) ∪ A'_d(G, A^k)); A^{k+1} = P-Attr(U^{k+1});
  A(G,d) = ∩_k A^k. A greatest fixpoint over O(n) rounds of ordinary attractors.
- Algorithm 1 (p.6): repeatedly compute A for the opponent's max priority d̄, remove it if non-empty; else compute
  A for the top priority d̂ and remove it. Claimed O(n^2 (n+m)) (Thm 7). Soundness rests on Thm 2 (A(G,d) is a
  P-dominion); completeness/progress rests on Lemma 3 (the minimal dominion is inside A(G, d̃)) and Lemma 4.
- Fidelity notes for L-COMP and REFEREE (typos, not findings): Alg. 1 line 6 tests "d" before any d is set
  (intended d̂ in context); the text cites "Theorem 3/4/6" for what are labelled Lemma 3/4/6; the A* formula is
  named "Astar" without the player annotation in its display; pdftotext drops the overbars (opponent marks),
  so implement from the rendered PDF, not from extracted text.

## 2. Which family it belongs to
L-LIT's classification, with the evidence:
- Family: attractor-based **dominion detection by greatest fixpoint, with no recursion** — i.e. a member of the
  *polynomial-time partial solver* family of Huth, Kuo, Piterman (fatal attractors; arXiv 1405.0386 v2, §3-§4),
  claimed to be complete. Closest relative: **psolQ** (HKP Fig. 5, Def. 6-7): start from all vertices X of one
  parity up to a bound b, compute an attractor that respects the priority layering (A* plays the role of HKP's
  "permissive monotone" layering), shrink X to the part that is re-attracted, iterate to a greatest fixpoint,
  remove the attractor of the result, repeat. HKP Thm 5: psolB, psol run in O(|V|^2 |E|), psolQ in O(|V|^2 |E| |c|).
  The claimed running time O(n^2 (n+m)) is exactly the psol/psolB bound.
- Same broader family (dominion search by attractors, only determined regions removed): priority promotion
  (Benerecetti, Dell'Erba, Mogavero, CAV 2016, LNCS 9780 pp. 270-290; FMSD 52(2) 2018) and tangle learning (van Dijk, CAV 2018, arXiv 1804.01023).
  Both are complete but exponential; HKP-type solvers are polynomial but incomplete (HKP Fig. 6 is an 8-vertex
  one-player game that psol, psolB and psolQ do not modify at all, §4.3 Example 6).
- Consequence for testing: Algorithm 1 is polynomial *by construction* (O(n) outer rounds × O(n) attractor rounds).
  Hard families cannot make it slow; **they can only make it wrong**. The two failure signatures to log are
  (a) an iteration of Algorithm 1 where both A(G, d̄) and A(G, d̂) are empty with V ≠ ∅ (Lemma 4 fails; as written
  the while-loop then never terminates), and (b) a removed A(G,d) that is not a dominion of its player
  (Thm 2 fails), detected as disagreement with Zielonka or a failed strategy check. Report per instance which
  of (a)/(b) occurred and in which round; that hands the REFEREE the lemma to look at.
- Barrier position: none of the quasi-polynomial lower-bound frameworks (separating automata, universal-tree
  attractor decomposition) is needed to judge it; the relevant prior evidence is the incompleteness of the
  psol family and the hard families for attractor-based dominion finders. Full barrier map: docs/lit/BARRIERS.md (T3).
