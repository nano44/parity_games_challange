# S3: steepest single switch (re-evaluated gain), and the most-vertices-improved variant

Status: UNDER TEST (2026-10-15).

## The rule, exactly
Valuations as in S1. For every improving edge e = (v, w) of σ compute σ[e] (σ with v -> w) and its valuation Ξ_{σ[e]}.
`steepest`: switch the e whose Ξ_{σ[e]}(v) is ≻-maximal (ties: smallest v, then w).
`most-improved`: switch the e maximizing |{x : Ξ_{σ[e]}(x) ≻ Ξ_σ(x)}| (ties as above).
Deterministic, memoryless, one switch per iteration. Start: best-reward. Implementation: `Engine.rule_steepest`,
`Engine.rule_most_improved`.

## Cost per iteration at bit cost
|improving edges| ≤ m valuations, each O(n^2 m) word operations: O(n^2 m^2) per iteration. Polynomial, but the
largest per-iteration cost of the candidates.

## Why it should be outside the barriers, before testing
- B8/B9: as S2 (valuation-based single switch, memoryless, not index-based). `steepest` is the parity analogue of the
  largest-increase rule; DLMM 2512.16684 Theorem 1.2 covers largest-increase for MDPs/LPs only.
- Friedmann 1106.0778 p.7 warns that one-step lookahead does not compose ("intransitivity of optimality of strategy
  updates"): the globally optimal multi-switch (switch-best) is exponential (Thm 8.4). A one-step *single* switch with
  true re-evaluation is not switch-best (which optimizes over all arena strategies at once) and is not covered by Thm 8.4.
- B11-B14: not applicable.

## Unproven assumptions, named
- A1: greedy on the true one-step gain avoids the counter's reset structure. (Friedmann's remark is evidence against.)
- A2: tie-breaking by index does not matter asymptotically.

## Kill criteria
As S2.
