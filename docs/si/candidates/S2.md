# S2: significance-ranked single switch (MSF: most significant first; LSF: least significant first)

Status: UNDER TEST (2026-10-15).

## The rule, exactly
Valuations and arena as in S1 (Friedmann 1106.0778 section 3). An improving edge (v, w): v ∈ V_E, Ξ_σ(σ(v)) ≺ Ξ_σ(w).
The *level* of an improving edge is the relevance position at which the two valuations first differ:
  (3, max rank of the two cycle nodes) if the cycle nodes differ; else (2, rank of the most relevant vertex in the
  symmetric difference of the two path sets); else (1, 0) (only the lengths differ).
MSF switches exactly one edge per iteration: an improving edge of maximal level (ties: smallest v, then smallest w).
LSF: minimal level, same tie-break. Memoryless, deterministic. Start: best-reward. Implementation: `Engine.rule_single_level`.

## Cost per iteration at bit cost
One valuation (O(n^2 m) word operations, see S1) plus O(m) level computations, each O(n) bits (a highest-set-bit of an
n-bit XOR). Polynomial; no hidden work.

## Why it should be outside the barriers, before testing
- B8: not switch-all (single switch), not switch-best, not Zadeh (no counters), not Cunningham (no round robin).
- B9 (DLMM 2512.16684 Thm 4.3 / Thm 1.1): the rule ranks improving edges by *valuation content* (where the valuations differ),
  not by global edge index; it is memoryless, so it is a "ranking-based rule" in DLMM's Def. 3.4 sense but not an
  index-based one. DLMM's Theorem 1.2 (rankings by index, reduced cost and objective improvement) is proved for MDPs/LPs,
  not for parity games; MSF is the parity analogue of a reduced-cost (Dantzig-like) ranking, so L-SI expects the
  analogy to bite, but no theorem covers it.
- B11-B14: not symmetric, not a prediction result, not LCP, not abstract.
- Design motive: every known exponential family is a binary counter whose high bits reset low bits (Friedmann 1106.0778
  section 5, DLMM Fig. 4.1). MSF always works on the most relevant open difference, LSF on the least relevant; each breaks
  the "increase the counter bit by bit" discipline in one direction. A new gadget may restore it; that is what the
  adversarial search looks for.

## Unproven assumptions, named
- A1: no sink-game gadget can make MSF (resp. LSF) re-do work at a fixed level exponentially often.
- A2: single switches do not lose the polynomial-iteration property that switch-all's "all at once" might give on some
  families (switch-all is itself exponential, B8, so this is not a loss against a known bound).
